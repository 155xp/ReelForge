# Research: copy proven formats, then do them better

New channels grow fastest on formats that are already pulling views. Before the first video, before each new
batch of subjects, and whenever views drop, look at what's working in the niche right now, then make your version
better and add the channel's twist. Don't invent untested premises.

Save each scan as `research/<topic>-<yyyy-mm-dd>.md` and put the takeaways in the channel bible's "Research
notes".

## A scan, step by step

1. **Find the videos.** Search the platform for the niche's phrases and hashtags (e.g. TikTok discover pages for
   "what if simulation", "3d simulation"), plus every creator the channel bible names. Aim for 30-50 videos from
   the last few weeks.
2. **Record the numbers** for each: views, likes, comments, shares, saves, the creator's follower count, the post
   date, the length, voiceover or not, and the title/hook. Share rate (shares / views) is the strongest sign a
   video is spreading; a big view count from a small account means the *format* carries, not the creator.
3. **Group them by format** ("X doubles every 2 seconds", "X disappeared for 5 seconds", disaster POV, "everyone
   on Earth did X"...) and note where each format is in its life: rising (a few creators, big numbers, copies from
   small accounts doing well), peaking, or saturated (many copies of the same subjects getting little).
4. **Break down the best 3-4 videos** second by second: sample a frame every 2-5 s (`ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -vf
   fps=1/3 f_%03d.png` on a downloaded copy you only use for study) and write the beats: the hook, when "you" enter,
   the trigger, each escalation step, the payoff, the ending, how long each text line stays up, what the counter
   shows. Note the sound: ambience, hits, the climax.
5. **Decide:** which format to lead with, the subjects that haven't been done (or were done badly), and what will
   make the channel's version better: sharper visuals, truer numbers, a stronger ending, a signature twist.

## What tends to work (October 2026, the what-if / simulation niche)

- Planet-scale questions everyone has already wondered about outperform local ones; repeats of a subject and pure
  fiction fall off hard.
- Series labels ("Ep. 5") and requests from comments bring viewers back.
- 60-90 s videos (TikTok's Creator Rewards needs over a minute); text-only videos still work when the visuals carry.
- Copycats arrive within days of a hit, so a recognisable signature (an ending, a counter style, a look) matters.

These go stale fast: trust a fresh scan over this list.

## Rules

- Study, don't copy assets: never reuse another creator's footage, audio or graphics. Download videos only to study
  them, and delete them afterwards.
- Every number in a video still needs a real source in its `facts.md`.
